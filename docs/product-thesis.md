# Neighbours Club — Product Thesis

**Status:** Founder strategy doc. The foundational statement of what the platform
is for. Anchors product decisions and the "compass test" for every feature.
**Companion to:** docs/go-to-market-thesis.md (the HOW-we-win) — this is the WHY.

---

## 0. The question this answers

"What is the most fundamental problem that Neighbours Club solves for a resident
of Kanata?"

The wrong answers are "food delivery," "local news," or "community points" — those
are features. Answering at the feature level loses the thread of what the platform
is actually for.

---

## 1. The fundamental problem

**The local economy and the local commons have been captured by extractive
intermediaries, and the resident has no trustworthy alternative.**

- When a Kanata resident orders food, DoorDash/Uber Eats take 15-30% from the
  restaurant they love, fund nothing back into the neighbourhood, and design the
  experience to manipulate them.
- When they want to know what's happening locally, they get an algorithmic feed
  optimized for engagement and outrage, or a thinning local-news landscape.
- The money and the attention both flow OUT of Kanata to people who don't live
  there and don't care about it. The resident is treated as a unit of extraction —
  and they can feel it even if they can't name it.

---

## 2. What Neighbours Club fundamentally offers

**A way for residents to participate in their own neighbourhood's economy and
information life without being extracted from or manipulated.**

That is the root. Everything built is a specific expression of it:

- The rejection of dark patterns **is the product**, not a constraint on it.
- The CP economy keeps value circulating locally instead of leaking to a national
  platform.
- The Notes editorial firewall is a promise that local information is diligent and
  honest, not engagement-bait.
- Group-buy and merchant economics are about merchants keeping their margins.

The whole thing answers: "what if the local platform were actually on the
resident's side?"

---

## 3. Why the demographic makes the thesis work

Kanata's tech-literate residents are precisely the people who can DETECT the
extraction and manipulation in the incumbents, and who would value an alternative
that demonstrably doesn't do it.

Trustworthiness isn't a nice-to-have — it's the entire competitive moat. Anyone
can build food delivery. Almost no one can credibly promise NOT to manipulate you,
because their business model depends on it. Ours is structured so it doesn't have
to.

---

## 4. The critical distinction: north-star problem vs. acquisition pitch

The fundamental problem we SOLVE and the immediate reason someone SIGNS UP are
different things. Conflating them is how you build something philosophically
beautiful that no one adopts.

- **North-star problem (the depth):** de-extraction, a trustworthy local commons.
  This is the moat and the compass.
- **Acquisition pitch (the surface):** cheaper food, useful local info, no
  dark-pattern annoyance. This is what a real resident actually says when asked
  why they use it.

**Lead marketing with the surface; build the product on the depth.** Know which
is which.

---

## 5. The one-sentence compression (for an investor or partner)

"Neighbours Club is the local commerce-and-community platform that keeps Kanata's
money and attention inside Kanata, structured so it can credibly promise residents
it will never manipulate them — which is the one thing the incumbents structurally
cannot offer."

---

## 6. The compass test

This thesis is the test every future feature must pass: does this keep value/trust
local and resist extraction, or does it drift toward the thing we define ourselves
against?

- The Notes editorial firewall PASSES (diligence + honesty = the trust promise).
- The CP non-clawback guarantee PASSES (don't punish good-faith users).
- Countdown-timer demurrage FAILED — it manufactured anxiety to drive behavior,
  the exact extraction/manipulation we exist to reject, even dressed in
  "transparency."

When a proposal is evaluated, this is the line: adopt anything that genuinely helps
on the merits, even if it changes course — but never a mechanic whose function is
to manipulate users against their interest. That line holds not because it's a
rule, but because violating it destroys the only thing the incumbents can't copy.
