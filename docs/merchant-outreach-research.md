# Merchant Outreach — Research Questions & Tracking

Purpose: every founding-merchant conversation does DOUBLE DUTY — build the
relationship AND gather the data the take-rate decision needs. This is the bridge
between the outreach (marketing chat) and the economics (take-rate decision).

## Framing principle
You're not a vendor pitching — you're a LOCAL FOUNDER asking for their help
understanding the market you both live in. That makes even pointed questions (what
do you pay Uber Eats?) feel collaborative, not salesy — and it's true. Fits the
brand: honest, local, early-stage, on their side.

## Two hard constraints (never break these)
- NO specific launch date — say "launching soon" / "founding partner."
- NO specific take-rate/commission quoted — value is qualitative ("keep more of
  your margin," "built to not gouge you"). Let their answers INFORM your rate;
  don't announce one.

---

## The questions (a MENU, not a script — pick 3-5 per conversation, follow the flow)

### Group 1 — Their current pain (opens the conversation, builds rapport)
- "Are you on the delivery apps right now — Uber Eats, DoorDash, Skip?"
- "How's that been working out for you?" (let them vent — complaints = your spec)
- "What's the hardest part of delivery for your business right now?"

### Group 2 — The economics (YOUR take-rate research — ask AFTER rapport)
- "Do you mind if I ask — roughly what do the big apps take on each order?"
  *** THE key question. Their answer sets your take-rate ceiling. ***
- "Does that number actually work for you, or is it just the cost of being visible?"
- "If a local platform charged meaningfully less, would that matter to you — or is
  the fee not really the issue?" (tests whether price is actually the wedge)

### Group 3 — Fit & commitment (gauge interest without overpromising)
- "We're building something local — Kanata-focused, launching soon, looking for a
  small group of founding restaurants. Want to hear more when we're closer?"
- "What would make you say yes to trying a new platform — and what would make you
  say no?" (yes = onboarding checklist; no = objections to preempt)

### Group 4 — Operational reality (feeds dispatch/density planning)
- "When are your busiest times — and your slow nights?" (slow nights = group-buy /
  promo value; also flatters them)
- "Roughly how many delivery orders do you do in a week?" (volume for economics
  modeling; identifies density drivers)

---

## How to run it
- Don't ask all 11 — that's an interrogation. 3-5 per chat, based on flow.
- ORDER: pain first (rapport) -> economics second (once they trust you) ->
  commitment third (once warm). Never lead cold with "what do you pay Uber Eats."
- Aim for "two locals talking shop," not a survey.

## Caveat on the take-rate data
Merchants may not know their exact rate or may round/lowball it. Uber Eats'
EFFECTIVE take (commission + fees + ad spend) is often higher than the headline
commission they'll quote. Treat answers as DIRECTIONAL, not precise — "most say
~25-30%" is the signal you want. Even directional, it sets your ceiling.
